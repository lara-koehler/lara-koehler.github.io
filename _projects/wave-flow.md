Early embryos rely on rhythmic biochemical "waves" sweeping through the cytoplasm to coordinate cell division. We found that these waves don't just pass through passively — they physically drag material along with them, creating coherent flows. We built a simple model of growing, wave-synchronized particles that reproduces this coupling, and showed that when the mechanical push is strong enough, it can even help different parts of the cell-like extract fall into sync with each other.
