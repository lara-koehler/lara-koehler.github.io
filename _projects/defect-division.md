In systems where units divide (embryos, tissues, bacterial colonies), you'd expect chemical rhythms to either sync up perfectly or fall apart into chaos. We found a third option: division actively maintains small, stable pockets of disorder ("phase defects") that would otherwise disappear over time. This gives a simple, physical explanation for why these systems keep a certain amount of built-in messiness instead of becoming perfectly regular — and it depends entirely on how synchronized things were to begin with.
